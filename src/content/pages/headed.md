## Progress so far

The picture is not all bad. This page describes what was funded and what changed. It does not judge which governments did it.

**Endometriosis has a national plan.** In December 2017 the federal Health Minister publicly apologised to women living with endometriosis ([Women's Agenda, 2021](https://womensagenda.com.au/latest/endometriosis-four-years-since-greg-hunts-apology-whats-changed/)), and in July 2018 the Department of Health published the first National Action Plan for Endometriosis ([Department of Health, 2018](https://australianwomenshealth.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/national-action-plan-for-endometriosis.pdf)). By early 2024, endometriosis-specific funding had reached $87.2 million across awareness, clinical care and research ([MJA InSight+, 2024](https://insightplus.mja.com.au/2024/2/why-australia-needs-a-second-national-action-plan-for-endometriosis/)). Specialised endometriosis and pelvic pain clinics now operate in general practice, and 11 more were funded in 2025–26, bringing the total to 33 and adding support for menopause and perimenopause ([RANZCOG, 2025](https://ranzcog.edu.au/news/government-invests-in-womens-health/)).

**A national women's health package.** The 2025–26 federal budget included a $792.9 million package for women's health ([Minister for Women, 2026](https://ministers.pmc.gov.au/white/one-year-millions-saved-cheaper-medicines-australian-women)). Among the changes:

- **New medicines on the PBS.** Newer contraceptive pills, including Yaz, Yasmin and the progestogen-only pill Slinda, were listed from 2025, along with three menopausal hormone therapies. Ryeqo, a newer treatment for endometriosis, was listed from 1 May 2025; it previously cost around $2,700 a year ([RANZCOG, 2025](https://ranzcog.edu.au/news/government-invests-in-womens-health/)).
- **New Medicare items.** From 1 July 2025, GPs can claim a Medicare rebate for a menopause and perimenopause health assessment of at least 20 minutes, once a year ([Best Practice Software, 2025](https://kb.bpsoftware.net/bppremier/oxford/Library/FAQs/MenopauseHealthAssessments.htm)). Medicare payments for bulk-billed IUD and contraceptive implant insertion and removal increased by up to 150% ([RANZCOG, 2025](https://ranzcog.edu.au/news/government-invests-in-womens-health/)).
- **Early results.** In its first year, more than 805,000 women filled more than 3 million cheaper scripts for the new contraceptives, menopausal hormone therapies and endometriosis treatment, and more than 8,500 women with endometriosis used Ryeqo ([Minister for Women, 2026](https://ministers.pmc.gov.au/white/one-year-millions-saved-cheaper-medicines-australian-women)). These figures come from a government media release.

**Victoria asked women about their pain.** Victoria's Inquiry into Women's Pain heard from more than 13,000 women, girls, carers, health professionals and researchers. Its 2025 report, *Bridging the Gender Pain Gap*, found that 90% of respondents had lived with pain for longer than a year and 54% lived with pain every day, and it made 27 recommendations across research, training, models of care and access ([Victorian Department of Health, 2025](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/public-health/inquiry-into-womens-pain)).

**Research is starting to count women properly.** In July 2024 the NHMRC and the federal Department of Health published a joint statement asking researchers to consider sex and gender in health and medical research ([NHMRC and Department of Health, 2024](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-07/statement-on-sex-gender-variations-of-sex-characteristics-and-sexual-orientation-in-health-and-medical-research.pdf)). After an update in late 2025, it became a requirement: from 2026, NHMRC and MRFF-funded research must consider sex and gender at all stages ([NHMRC, 2026](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/research-policy/gender-equity/statement-sex-and-gender-health-and-medical-research)). The next test is whether it is followed and measured.

**What's still missing.** No published national estimate was found of how many Australians live with PMDD. The main study of PMOS in Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander women is more than a decade old. And many of the figures on this site come from surveys or from studies that are now dated, because newer, larger Australian studies haven't been done yet.
