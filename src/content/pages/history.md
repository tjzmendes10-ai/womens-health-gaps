title: How we got here
description: A timeline of how women came to be explained away, left out of medical research, and slowly written back in.
---

The gaps in women's health today didn't appear by accident, and they aren't unique to any one country. For most of the history of modern medicine, research, testing and funding treated men as the default.

The effects are still measurable. In a 2024 global analysis of 183 widely used treatments, based on more than 650 research papers, only half had results reported separately for women and men. The same report estimated that closing the women's health gap could add at least US$1 trillion a year to the global economy by 2040 ([World Economic Forum and McKinsey Health Institute, 2024](https://www.weforum.org/reports/closing-the-women-s-health-gap-a-1-trillion-opportunity-to-improve-lives-and-economies)).

For centuries, women's symptoms were blamed on the womb. In the twentieth century, a well-meant urge to protect pregnancies helped keep women out of drug trials, and laboratory research defaulted to male animals on the assumption that female hormones would make results messy. Each of those ideas has since been tested, challenged or reversed. This timeline traces how, with Australia's part in the story alongside the international milestones.
