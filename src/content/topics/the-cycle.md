The menstrual cycle affects far more than fertility. It shapes mood, energy, pain and long-term health, yet problems linked to it are routinely dismissed as "just part of being a woman".

In a 2023 national survey of more than 3,500 Australian women aged 18 to 44, nearly 4 in 5 reported painful, heavy or irregular periods. Of those, 3 in 4 said it made daily activities harder, and 2 in 5 had missed work or study because of it ([Jean Hailes for Women's Health, 2025](https://www.jeanhailes.org.au/articles/new-report-reveals-serious-impact-of-periods-on-daily-life/)). That was a survey, not a clinical study, but it shows how ordinary this kind of disruption has become.
