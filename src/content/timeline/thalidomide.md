Thalidomide was prescribed to ease morning sickness in early pregnancy. In December 1961, Sydney obstetrician William McBride reported in *The Lancet* that babies born to women who had taken it were being born with severe malformations, including missing limbs. In Germany, paediatrician Widukind Lenz reached the same conclusion. The drug was withdrawn. The disaster left a deep fear of exposing pregnancies to untested medicines, a fear that shaped the decades that followed.
